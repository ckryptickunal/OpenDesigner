# 4 UI Design Hacks to KILL boring designs

## Metadata

- Video ID: `SfX43uIubj4`
- Channel: Kole Jain

## Summary

Kole Jain shows four ways to turn a sterile, corporate-looking landing page into a playful one: add context elements, add motion, bring the text to life, and add finishing touches such as a fun 404 page. Context elements are illustrations around a centered message that tell people what the product is for without reading, as long as there is enough space around the text that they read as context and not clutter. Motion covers entrances that suit each element, easing, and parallax on scroll; text gets simple word animations and friendly, jargon-free copy. For a design system, the video gives a playfulness decision (how much decoration, which imagery style, how much motion) and a set of checks for when decoration tips into clutter.

## Key Ideas

- Many modern sites have traded fun and curiosity for a sterile, corporate look.
- Removing the extra elements from a busy landing page does not make it clearer; it makes it feel empty.
- Illustrations around the core message can tell people what the product is for before they read anything.
- Keep generous space around the text so surrounding elements read as context, not clutter.
- When elements break out of the grid, arrange them so they pull attention toward the center and thin out further from it.
- Pick an imagery style that matches the vibe: blobs and fun colors are most playful, realistic imagery is more professional, doodles sit in the middle.
- Things that do not need to move can still move; each decorative element can get its own entrance instead of a robotic fade.
- Elements placed in large margins can use parallax on scroll to make a page feel more lifelike without heavy backgrounds.
- Text is most of a website, so simple animations on key words draw attention to it; the aim is attention, not complexity.
- Small hover interactions on almost everything keep a very simple site from feeling bland.
- Friendly, natural copy beats corporate jargon.
- Finishing touches people rarely see, like 404 pages, are a place to be quirky and fun.

### Process

1. Study reference sites with a similar layout: Look at how playful examples (an invoicing site, a crypto site) use elements around a centered message to give context.
2. Rebuild the hero: Remove the existing image, center the text, adjust the other styles, then add images with doodled illustrations, a few twinkles and a couple of tasteful scribbles.
3. Check for clutter: Keep good spacing around the text, resist filling the last empty space, and arrange off-grid elements so they draw the eye to the center and trail off away from it.
4. Match the vibe: Place the design on a scale from very playful (blobs, fun colors) through doodles to professional (realistic imagery).
5. Add entrances: Give each decorative element its own entrance (rotate and pop, fly in then bob, move and rotate in) and apply easing.
6. Add parallax: Use a bit of parallax on scroll for elements in the large margins to make the page feel more lifelike.
7. Animate the text: Line by line, animate key words: a simple fade-in slide-down, a word that becomes a progress bar that fills then rises out, dollar signs that bounce and slide down, and a checkbox at the end.
8. Rewrite the copy: Replace corporate phrases with friendly, natural language.
9. Add finishing touches: Design a quirky 404 page that ties in the personal copy and smooth animations.
