## Summary

Kole Jain argues that a well-made website that follows all the right principles can still be boring, and that a good design and an interesting one are different things. He walks through ways to make a site more interesting: vary the column layout from the wireframe stage, break the box with overlaps and elements that extend past the frame, treat photos (remove backgrounds, crop and zoom, overlay text, mask into shapes), use size for attention and variety, and add depth with offset elements and adjusted section backgrounds. He then covers changing the page background (dark mode with lighter accents and frosted glass, or off-whites and textured backgrounds) and adding simple motion such as photos slightly zooming in or out, accordions, marquee text and staggered entrances. He ends by reworking one of his own old portfolio designs with these ideas. For a design system, it offers a set of website layout, imagery, depth, background and motion choices, with the recurring point that interesting sites have more variety.

## Key Ideas

- A good design and an interesting design are different; a site can follow every principle and still be boring.
- Layout starts at the wireframe: a boring, average wireframe usually leads to a boring site.
- Boring sites repeat the same column layout, namely image on one side and text on the other; mixing in a full-screen section, cards and three columns already makes it look much better.
- Breaking the box means elements overlap or extend outside the frame instead of lining up perfectly in columns and rows.
- Start breaking the box with small changes, such as shifting cards up and down to add a feeling of movement.
- Images make up a significant portion of a website, so their quality can make or break it; AI images are an option but don't fit every circumstance.
- Image treatments that make a layout more unique: remove the background of a single-object photo, crop and zoom into a product image, overlay text on a wide shot, or mask images into shapes that match the site's style.
- Making an element bigger draws attention to it and adds variety; interesting websites have more variety in layouts, colors and sizes.
- Stacking a two-column horizontal layout into a one-column vertical one, with larger elements, keeps focus on one feature at a time.
- Depth does not have to come from shadows: offset buttons on backgrounds or images, sections with adjusted backgrounds, and a dark background behind lighter cards also add depth.
- Dark mode won't suit every site but should work on most modern-themed ones; it is more than a new background: text flips to white and over-saturated primary and accent colors need lighter versions.
- Replacing plain white with off-whites, or an image background with a noise overlay, adds color, texture and cohesion without a change as drastic as dark mode.
- Motion is one of the most foolproof ways to make a site feel unique and alive, but sections like marquee text come with reduced usability.

### Process

1. Wireframe first: Start with a wireframe and make sure the layout itself is varied, since a boring wireframe leads to a boring site.
2. Vary the layouts: Replace repeated image-and-text rows with a mix of full-screen sections, cards and different column counts.
3. Break the box: Outlining everything with boxes shows whether anything overlaps; introduce overlaps, shift cards up and down, or let a card section extend outside the frame.
4. Treat the visuals: Use high-quality images and apply a treatment suited to each photo: background removal, crop and zoom, overlay text, or shape masks.
5. Use size: Make key elements bigger for attention and variety; consider stacking features into one column.
6. Add depth: Use shadows, offset buttons on backgrounds or images, and sections with adjusted backgrounds.
7. Rethink the background: Try dark mode (white text, lighter accents, frosted glass) or, for smaller changes, off-whites and textured backgrounds.
8. Add motion: Add simple movement such as photos slightly zooming in or out, accordions, marquee text or staggered entrances, weighing usability.
9. Build an offset-button hover in prototyping mode: Duplicate the frame, move the background in the copy, switch to prototyping mode, connect the two frames and add a hover effect.
10. Rework a portfolio design (example): Remove the image and stats, center the text and enlarge the name, bring the stats back rearranged, cut text to free vertical space and move everything up, show mobile screens below, then make them slide up in a staggered sequence on reload.
