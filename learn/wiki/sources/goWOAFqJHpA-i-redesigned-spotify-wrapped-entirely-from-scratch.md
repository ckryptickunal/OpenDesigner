# I Redesigned Spotify Wrapped Entirely From SCRATCH

## Metadata

- Video ID: `goWOAFqJHpA`
- Channel: Kole Jain
- Published: 2025-01-10T21:21:03Z
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goWOAFqJHpA

## Summary

Kole Jain redesigns the Spotify Wrapped mobile experience as a critique-then-rebuild exercise. He keeps the familiar highlight-reel format because people have used it for years, but switches from tapping through slides to scrolling (like Reddit Recap), moves the most important controls to the bottom of the screen where they are easier to reach, and makes the share button always visible. He splits the reel into four named chapters with a bottom dropdown chip for skipping, and gives each chapter a 'view summary' screen that works as a dashboard of shareable stats. He then plans features per chapter and replaces per-slide sharing with one button that offers share slide, share chapter or share wrapped. For a design system, the video is a worked example of mobile reach-based control placement, removing controls a gesture already covers, avoiding duplicate buttons, and evolving an established layout instead of replacing it.

## Key Ideas

- A click-through highlight reel is good for a first viewing with music and animation, but bad for checking stats at a quick glance.
- Offering both a highlight reel and a dashboard (as Apple Music does) gives more functionality and insight.
- Changing a layout people have used for years is risky, so evolve the existing format instead of replacing it.
- Scrolling through slides makes more sense than clicking through them.
- Important controls belong at the bottom of a phone screen, where they are easier to reach, even if pushing everything to the top looks good.
- The action a product succeeds on (sharing, for Wrapped) should be immediately and always visible.
- A control can be removed when the audience already knows a gesture that does the same job, such as tap to pause.
- Long sequences become easier to use when split into named chapters with a way to skip straight to one.
- A per-chapter summary screen can act as a dashboard without leaving the story format.
- Two similar buttons on one screen confuse people; one button that opens a few clear options is simpler.
- A personal stat can carry a short comparison with last year, or a friendly note when something is new.
- People prefer being placed into a few recognizable categories over hyper-specific AI-generated labels.
- Shared content should show whose it is, so the recipient does not confuse it with their own.

### Rules and practices

- **should** (layout, all): Place the most important controls (share, volume, chapter navigation) at the bottom of a phone screen, not pushed to the top. Why: The bottom of the screen is easier to reach, even though pushing everything to the top looks good. [at the bottom of the screen where it's easier to reach]
- **consider** (layout, web): On the mobile web, check that the browser bar does not get in the way of navigation placed at the bottom of the screen; an app-only product does not have this problem. Why: Spotify Wrapped only works in the app, unlike Apple Music which is accessible online, so the redesign does not need to worry about a browser bar covering its bottom navigation. [browser bar getting in the way of our navigation]
- **should** (components, all): Keep the action the product's success depends on (here, share) immediately and always visible. Why: Sharing is the reason Wrapped is so successful, yet the original layout did not always show the share button. [why the share button isn't immediately and always visible]
- **consider** (components, all): Automatically pause playing video when the share button is tapped. Why: The author builds this into the new share button without giving a reason; presumably so the video does not keep playing while the person shares [inferred]. [that will automatically pause the video when it's clicked]
- **consider** (components, all): Don't add a visible pause button when the audience already knows tap-to-pause from social media platforms; keep the close X at the top and move volume to the bottom. Why: Wrapped is aimed at Gen Z, who know tap-to-pause from every social platform, so the button is redundant. [do we really need this button]
- **should** (patterns, all): Don't make people click through every slide to see their stats; let them scroll, and give each chapter a summary screen that shows most stats at once. Why: Click-through slides are the worst setup for checking stats at a quick glance. [when it comes to looking at stats at a quick glance]
- **should** (process, all): Don't entirely replace a layout people have used for years; evolve it instead. Why: After about eight years people are used to the format, so a full layout change is probably not the best idea. [entirely changing the layout after like eight strong years]
- **consider** (components, all): For a story sequence that includes video, put the navigation on the right and extend it into the progress bar. Why: The redesign copies Reddit Recap, which scrolls with navigation on the right; since some slides are videos, the navigation is expanded into their progress bar. [let's move our navigation to the right]
- **consider** (patterns, all): Split a long sequence into named chapters and put a dropdown chip at the bottom of the screen so people can skip to the part they want. Why: So people can easily skip to the part they want. Values: four chapters. [put these into a drop- down ship at the bottom of the screen]
- **should** (patterns, all): Give each chapter a 'view summary' button that opens a dashboard of that chapter's stats, shareable without leaving the format. Why: It keeps the story format while making most stats readily visible and easy to share. [a button of view summary it'll act as our dashboard]
- **should** (components, all): Don't place two similar share buttons on one screen; use one share button that opens a short list of scopes (share slide, share chapter, share wrapped). Why: A separate chapter share button next to the existing one could be confusing. Values: three options. [you get three options share slide share chapter or share wrapped]
- **should** (patterns, all): When someone shares a whole collection of personal content, show the owner's name at the top of what the recipient sees. Why: So the recipient does not get confused about whether it is theirs or the sender's. [will see your name at the top]
- **consider** (content, all): Add a short comparison line under a headline stat, such as the percentage change from last year, or a friendly note like 'you two just met' when the item is new. Why: The author wants the top-artist stat to show change: this year compared with last, or a note when the artist is brand new. Values: 2200 minutes, 1,000, 120% increase. [add some text below that compares this year to last]
- **should** (content, all): Don't label users with hyper-specific AI-generated categories; place them into a few recognizable categories. Why: The AI-generated genres were not well received by people who wanted to be classified into a few select categories. [represent genres with weird AI garbage]
- **consider** (patterns, all): For a month-by-month timeline, let people swipe through every month rather than showing only some. Why: If only some months are shown, there is no point to the timeline. [give them the option to swipe through each month]

Why: Fruit is universally known, easy to represent with emojis, quirky, and simple enough to share easily. [most fruit is pretty universally known very easy to represent]

### Decisions it informs

- What format should a stats recap (like a year in review) use?
  - Click-through highlight reel: Slides with background music and animation; people click through to each next slide. When: A first, watch-it-all-through viewing.
  - Dashboard: Most stats visible at once, quick to scan and share. When: Checking or showing off stats at a quick glance.
  - Both reel and dashboard: More functionality and insight, as Apple Music does. When: When you can offer both without breaking a format people already know [inferred].
  - Scrolling reel with chapters and per-chapter summary: Keeps the familiar reel but people scroll instead of tapping, skip by chapter, and open a summary dashboard per chapter. When: An established reel format that needs quick-glance access without a full layout change.
  - Recommendation: Keep the reel people have used for years, switch to scrolling like Reddit Recap, add chapters with a skip dropdown, and give each chapter a 'view summary' dashboard.
- Where should the key controls sit on a phone screen: top or bottom?
  - Top: Pushing everything to the top looks good, but controls are far away and harder to reach. When: Not recommended for important controls.
  - Bottom: Share, volume and chapter navigation are within easy reach. When: Important controls, especially in an app-only product with no browser bar in the way.
  - Recommendation: Bottom, because it is easier to reach; the close X can stay at the top.
- Should a story-style video screen have a visible pause button?
  - Visible pause button: An extra on-screen control that takes space. When: Audiences that may not know the tap-to-pause convention [inferred].
  - Tap to pause, no button: Fewer controls on screen; the pause gesture matches social media stories. When: Audiences like Gen Z who know tap-to-pause from every social platform.
  - Recommendation: Remove the pause button for a Gen Z audience and rely on tap-to-pause.
- How should sharing work when content has several levels (slide, chapter, whole recap)?
  - Share each slide individually: Only single slides can be shared. When: Not recommended; the author says it kind of sucks.
  - Separate share buttons per level: A chapter share button next to the slide share button. When: Not recommended; two buttons could be confusing.
  - One share button with three options: Tapping share opens share slide, share chapter or share wrapped. When: Content with nested levels that people want to share at different sizes.
  - Recommendation: One share button that opens three options, with the sender's name shown at the top of shared chapters or full recaps.
- How should users be categorized in a personalized summary?
  - Hyper-specific AI-generated labels: Unique but strange category names that people did not relate to. When: Not recommended; it was not well received.
  - A few select familiar categories: Recognizable, easy to understand and share, but less specific. When: When people want to be classified rather than be unique.
  - Other lenses on taste: Most consistent artists across the year, month-by-month journey, or playful symbols like a fruit pie chart. When: To add personalization without odd labels.
  - Recommendation: Use a few familiar categories (top genres) and add personalization through other features such as most consistent artists and a month-by-month journey.

### Process

1. Critique the current version: List what users are unhappy about: features that were removed (city match, audio auras, top genres) and features that were added but disliked (AI-generated genres).
2. Decide the format: Weigh what the current format is good at (background music, animation) against what it is bad at (quick-glance stats); look at comparable products (Apple Music, Reddit Recap) and avoid changing a layout people are used to.
3. Break down the layout: Ask why the key action is not always visible and why controls are far away; move important controls to the bottom for reach and remove controls a known gesture already covers.
4. Design the navigation: Split the content into chapters, add a dropdown chip at the bottom to skip between them, and give each chapter a summary dashboard.
5. Plan features per chapter: Keep the classics, restore well-liked features, and add new ones per chapter (most skipped song, late night anthem, rising star, your obsession, fruit pie chart, month-by-month journey).
6. Design sharing: Replace per-slide sharing with one share button offering slide, chapter or whole-recap scopes, and label shared recaps with the owner's name.
