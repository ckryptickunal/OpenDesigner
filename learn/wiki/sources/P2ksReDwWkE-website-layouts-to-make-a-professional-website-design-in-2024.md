# Website Layouts To Make A Professional Website Design in 2024

## Metadata

- Video ID: `P2ksReDwWkE`
- Channel: Kole Jain
- Published: 2024-03-19T06:00:09Z
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2ksReDwWkE

## Summary

Kole Jain walks through nine website section layouts he calls trending in 2024. For most of them he shows real websites (or Dribbble shots) that use it, then his Figma wireframe and a finished design; the ninth has no live example. The sections are: cards on a dark background, a thin cropped photo with profound text, giant headline text over a huge image, a scrollable multi-image layout, stacked cards that slide up on scroll, a horizontal card carousel, a bento grid, an image with cut-out lettering, and a text-only section with lots of white space. Each section comes with practical notes: keep spacing precise, put a gradient under text on photos, crop and pick images carefully, match the layout to how much content you have, and give text-only sections enough body copy. For a design system, this is a catalogue of landing-page section patterns, with notes on when each fits, that could be offered to people as visual options.

## Key Ideas

- Cards sitting on a dark background work as a header and can carry a whole website's theme, but the cards must sit very close together with precise spacing.
- A thin, cropped photo with really profound text on it works as an interlude or spacer between text-heavy sections.
- Text placed on a photo needs a gradient under it, or it is hard to read.
- A giant-text hero gets its impact from the contrast between huge headline text and very small navbar text, and placing the navbar beneath the headline makes the headline feel more important.
- In the giant-text section, what really brings it together is the massive images, slideshow or video beneath the text.
- Images that are blown up large should be large, high-resolution photos so they do not look pixelated, and the thin photo band and the multi-image layout need heavy cropping, so check orientation and aspect ratio when choosing images.
- A multi-image layout can show one main large image with more to scroll through, and dot indicators help show how many images there are and how far along you are.
- Stacked cards that slide up over each other as you scroll suit a list of three to five options that need some detail.
- The horizontal card section is simple and useful but is not used on most websites.
- A horizontal card carousel shows a lot of information in a small space; it works for 3 to about 10 cards and should not rely only on small navigation buttons.
- Bento grid box sizes change as the content plan develops, and a bento grid needs enough content to fill it.
- A cut-out lettering effect over a big image is easy in Figma with subtracts, but the image should not have anything important in the corner the cut-out removes.
- A text-only section can open the page up with white space instead of filling the empty area with an image, but it needs three or more lines of body text so it does not feel too empty.

### Decisions it informs

- Which layout should this website section use?
  - Cards on a dark background: Rounded rectangles sit tightly together on a dark background; works as a header and as a site-wide theme. When: A header or a consistent look across the whole site, when you can keep spacing very precise.
  - Thin cropped photo with profound text: A very thin cropped photo with really profound text on it and a gradient under the text; acts as a spacer between sections. When: You have something really profound to say, or need a spacer between two text-heavy sections.
  - Giant text over a giant image: Massive display headline, small nav links beneath it, then a giant image, slideshow or video; the headline feels very important. When: You want the giant-text trend and have large, high-resolution photos, a slideshow or a video to blow up.
  - Multi-image scroll layout: One main large image plus more you scroll through with an arrow, with a bit of text; can grow each image as it scrolls past. When: You want to show multiple images and some text together.
  - Stacked scroll cards: Each card slides up over the previous one as you scroll, laying out steps of a process in front of the viewer. When: A list of three to five options or steps that need some detail.
  - Horizontal card carousel: A row of cards the viewer moves through with buttons; shows a lot of information in a small space. When: 3 to about 10 cards; more than 10 is cumbersome.
  - Bento grid: A compact grid of boxes of different sizes (Linear-style), full width or compact. When: You have a fair amount of text and image content to fill the boxes.
  - Cut-out image text: A big image with uppercase letters sitting in a cut-out; trendy on Dribbble. When: You have an image that fits the space with nothing important in the cut-out corner.
  - Text-only with white space: No photos; text leads and the empty area opens up the page. When: After several photo-heavy sections, when you have three or more lines of body text.

- How many items do you have to show, and which section pattern fits that count?

  - Horizontal card carousel: A row of cards moved through with buttons (or dragging); lots of information in a small space. When: Anywhere from 3 to about 10 cards.

### Process

1. Collect live examples: For each section type, find real websites (or Dribbble shots) that use it and note what makes it work.
2. Wireframe in Figma: Build a simple wireframe of the section's structure before designing it.
3. Design a finished version for a real use: Apply the wireframe to a specific site, such as a wine tour or vineyard site or a site called Evolve.
4. Let content reshape the layout: As the content plan firms up, adjust box sizes and splits; the bento top row became three boxes instead of two.
5. Choose and crop images for the layout: Pick high-resolution images whose orientation and aspect ratio suit the layout, expecting heavy cropping.
6. Critique and note improvements: Review the finished design for gaps, such as adding dot indicators to a multi-image layout.
