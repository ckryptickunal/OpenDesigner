# He Spent $1M on A/B Tests. Here's What Won.

## Metadata

## Summary

Mobbin interviews Vitaliy Urban, founder of the astrology app Moonly, about six years and over $1M of A/B tests on onboarding, login, paywalls, pricing and payments. Many winners go against common advice: a 30-screen onboarding built around the user's problem and tailored to the ad they came from, no login screen at all, one consistent paywall instead of contextual per-feature paywalls, realistic images instead of cartoon 3D characters, and no free trial. Small daily tests on every paywall element (images, titles, social proof, fine print, price units) took paid-traffic conversion from 10% in 2020 to 40%. For someone building a design system, it shows which onboarding and paywall patterns to treat as decisions to test rather than defaults, and that imagery style should match who is paying.

## Key Ideas

- A long onboarding (Moonly: 30 screens) is built around the user's job or pain: give a small bite-sized insight, then show how a feature solves the user's specific problem.
- Onboarding can be tailored to the ad and App Store page a person came from, so each user job gets its own flow.
- Answers collected in onboarding should personalize the first session, including the home screen's quick access buttons.
- Ask why an element such as a login screen is needed before copying it from other apps; removing Moonly's login reduced cognitive load.
- Contextual paywalls, one per feature, made users think every feature had to be bought separately; one consistent paywall that unlocks everything won.
- Paywall images were the biggest lever: a realistic image beat the Disney-style 3D characters the team liked, because paying users over 35 could not see themselves in cartoons.
- Personalizing images with the user's own face backfired; people are sensitive to anything involving their face.
- Paywall words alone raised conversion by 12%; the guest recommends always adding social proof, and clips from earlier Mobbin guests say a no-commitment fine print line also helps.
- A pricey lifetime plan can act as an anchor that raises perceived value; and because people compare prices to each other, not to value, Moonly kept the number and changed the unit (per month to per week).
- Free trials can give away the most valuable feature; Moonly removed its trial and uses a one-time offer, usually on day three.
- People share insights about themselves, not features, so a one-tap share flow on screenshot drives free reach.
- An AI guide giving personal advice needs to feel trustworthy, and a realistic look worked better than cartoon, Disney-like or faceless styles.
- Every element, icon and piece of text earned its place by winning a test, and AI tools help run hundreds of tests a month.
- Before building an expensive app, validate the market with a lean web funnel.

### Process

1. Question every element: Before adding something seen in other apps, such as a login screen, ask why the product needs it and what it is really for.
2. Onboarding around the job: Name the user's job or pain, give a bite-sized insight, then introduce the feature by showing how it solves their specific problem.
3. Connect ad to experience: Link each ad to a custom App Store product page, then to a matching onboarding flow and app experience.
4. Personalize the first session: Collect details during onboarding and use them for the home screen, including its quick access buttons.
5. Test every paywall element: Run small experiments on images, titles, subtitles, social proof, fine print and prices, keeping only what wins; improvements come almost every day.
6. Let AI reconcile the data: Use AI tools to match numbers across analytics sources and recommend ship, don't ship or collect more data.
7. Start lean with a web funnel: Get market signals from a web funnel built alone with AI agents or one front-end developer before building the full app; move slowly and cheaply.
