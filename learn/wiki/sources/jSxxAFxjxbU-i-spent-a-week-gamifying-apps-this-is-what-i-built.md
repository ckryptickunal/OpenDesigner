# I spent a week gamifying apps. This is what I built

## Metadata

- Video ID: `jSxxAFxjxbU`

## Summary

Kole Jain spends a week learning what gamification is, studies Duolingo as a case study, and then designs a gamified savings app called Fin Base in Figma. He names the key traits he found: gamification is more common than expected, it usually hinges on one central currency, it shows what you have done and what is left, it often has a social side such as leaderboards and badges, and its rewards only work when tied to real goals. His app turns saving into a game: users set goals, earn a currency called fin bits for putting money aside and completing goals, keep a streak, and climb a local leaderboard with promotion and demotion ranges. The onboarding shows the app's value (insights into past spending) before any gamification, and the product ends up as four tabs: goals, leaderboard, analytics and education. For a design system, it is a worked example of a retention pattern set (currency, streaks, leagues, badges, quizzes) rather than of visual tokens.

## Key Ideas

- Gamification is more common than it seems: LinkedIn turns profile completion into an achievement hunt and credit card points are gamified.
- Most gamification is built around one central currency (Duolingo XP, Todoist Karma) that shows progress and unlocks features.
- Good gamification makes visible what you have accomplished and what is still left, as Exercism does for a coding language.
- A social layer, such as leaderboards and public badges, lets people see how they stack up against friends.
- Rewards only motivate when they are tied to real goals; a meaningless reward is ignored.
- Duolingo layers XP (ranks you, unlocks quest chests), gems (a second currency for modifiers), hearts (lost on wrong answers), streaks and achievements.
- The creator skipped task managers and learning platforms because other companies had already gamified them very well, and chose money management instead.
- Show the app's value (insights into past spending) in onboarding before you start gamifying.
- Scale the reward to the size of the goal, so bigger goals earn more currency.
- In the money app, people are ranked on a local leaderboard to account for currency and income differences.
- A league leaderboard can have a promotion range, a neutral middle, and a demotion range below.
- An education section with quizzes that pay out the currency links learning back to the leaderboard.
- Challenges would be nice, but an education section on financial literacy was judged more important.
- Designing took far longer than planned: the four main screens took three whole days instead of four to six hours, partly because files kept getting deleted or misplaced.

### Process

1. Learn what gamification is: Research examples across apps (LinkedIn, credit card points, Todoist, Exercism, fitness apps) and distil the key traits.
2. Study one strong case: Break down a well-gamified app (Duolingo) into its activities, currencies, rewards, punishments, streaks and achievements, and check it against the traits.
3. Pick an under-served domain: Skip domains already gamified very well (task managers, learning platforms) and choose one that is not (money management).
4. Plan the system: Define how data comes in (bank connection), what users set (goals), what earns the currency (saving, completing goals), what the currency does (local leaderboard), plus streaks, virtual accounts, analytics, an education section and badges.
5. Wireframe onboarding: Welcome the user, ask them to connect their bank with reassurance, show swipeable spending insights, then ask for a savings goal (and any amount already saved) with a slider for the time period.
6. Pick a palette from a reference: Take inspiration from an existing palette (Wise) and add a color of your own (purple).
7. Review the next day: Look at the design again the next day and make bigger changes if needed (here, switching to dark mode).
8. Design the main tabs: Build the core screens: goal overview, leaderboard, spending and saving analytics, and education.
