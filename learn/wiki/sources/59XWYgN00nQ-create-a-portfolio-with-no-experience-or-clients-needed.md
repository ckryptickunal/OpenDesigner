# Create A Portfolio With No Experience (or clients) Needed

## Summary

Kole Jain explains how a designer with no clients can still build a portfolio by making realistic fake projects. The process is: get a unique prompt from a site such as Good Brief or Sharpen Design, make sure it fits the niche you want to work in, expand it into a full brief with ChatGPT, collect inspiration, then design with the brief in mind and no placeholder text. He compares where to show the work (Dribbble, Behance, Notion sites, a personal website) and says how many projects are enough. For someone building a design system this is mostly career advice, but the steps of starting from a written brief and using realistic content instead of lorem ipsum carry over to any design work [inferred].

## Key Ideas

- Without client work, a portfolio can be built from fake projects.
- A good fake project starts with a good idea; get a unique prompt from a site such as Good Brief or Sharpen Design.
- Pick project ideas that match the niche you want to end up working in.
- Making work for everyone targets no one; without a niche you are the expert of everything and therefore nothing.
- Choose a niche by what you are passionate about and whether products could reasonably be sold in it.
- Turn the prompt into a full project brief (the video uses ChatGPT) and keep it in mind while designing.
- Gather inspiration from Dribbble, Pinterest, Instagram or Behance before designing.
- Make the design as realistic as possible, with no lorem ipsum.
- Dribbble suits interfaces and Behance suits case studies; a personal website seems much more professional but costs a lot of time and energy; Notion sites are quick to set up and can be more personal than Dribbble or Behance.
- Two to three case studies, or three to four interface-only projects, are enough; focus on very high-quality work that shows off your skills.

2. Check the niche fit: Make sure the idea lines up with the niche you want to work in, chosen by passion and whether products could reasonably be sold in it.
3. Write a full brief: Expand the prompt into a full project brief; the video uses ChatGPT.
4. Gather inspiration: Collect references from Dribbble, Pinterest, Instagram, Behance or similar sites.
5. Design realistically: Create the design with the brief in mind, as realistic as possible, with no lorem ipsum.
6. Choose where to display it: Pick Dribbble (interfaces), Behance (case studies), a Notion website (quick) or a personal website (most professional).
7. Curate the set: Keep 2-3 case studies or 3-4 interface projects and focus on very high quality.
