# He Tested 4,700 Paywalls. These Won.

## Metadata

- Video ID: `E7RzEZ8GlHE`
- Channel: Mobbin
- Published: 2026-08-11T15:14:57Z

## Summary

Mobbin interviews Jonathan Parra, who has designed over 4,700 paywalls for consumer apps, about what to test on a subscription paywall and in what order. He says a first paywall only needs a headline, a couple of bullet points and a continue button, and that radically different designs move results most, followed by personalization, then how the products and prices are packaged in the footer, with device-based pricing as a late, costly lever. He argues the right success metric depends on the business: ARPU for a quick launch, LTV and retention for a long-term business, because high ARPU from weekly plans can hide very high churn. He covers paywall touch points (after onboarding, feature gates, settings, cancellation and deletion), account creation before the paywall, when steep discounts help or cannibalize revenue, and why he dislikes spin-the-wheel paywalls. For a design system this is guidance on the paywall pattern and its variants rather than on visual tokens [inferred].

## Key Ideas

- Paywall optimization matters little at the start; get users into the app first and optimize that traffic later.
- A first paywall can be just a headline describing the product, a couple of bullet points on what it does, and a continue button.
- Radically different paywall designs (video, bullet list, trial timeline, long-form) move results more than anything else, so test those first.
- After design, personalization matters most: the user's name, their goals and imagery that reflects their identity.
- Price packaging comes next: how the footer lays out the products and how each price is presented.
- Default to the yearly product for its higher LTV and show only two products to reduce cognitive load, while checking that yearly suits your business.
- Device-based pricing tests are among the last levers because they take so much work for smaller gains.
- ARPU alone can mislead; weekly plans can raise ARPU while monthly churn runs very high.
- Choose the key metric by the kind of business: ARPU for a quick launch, LTV and retention for a long-term business.
- The share of installs that see the paywall is a health check: above 80% is good, 90% or above is excellent.
- Run the current paywall as a control against four to five new designs, and be patient because wins are not guaranteed.
- Every touch point deserves testing, but the after-onboarding paywall is the most used.
- Steep discounts fit win-back moments for subscribers about to cancel, not the first paywall after onboarding.
- Spin-the-wheel discount paywalls can raise short-term revenue but teach users to close paywalls and wait for an offer.
- The highest converting paywall is not always the right one; it depends on what kind of business you are building.

### Decisions it informs

- What should you test first on a paywall?
  - Design: Radically different layouts, such as video, bullet list, trial timeline or long-form paywalls. When: First; design tests move the needle the most.
  - Personalization: The paywall calls out the user's name, highlights their goals and shows imagery representative of their identity. When: Second, once a design has won.
  - Price packaging: Changes to the footer: how the products are grouped, what goes in each group and how the price is presented. When: Third.
  - Device-based pricing: Different prices by device, network and battery state. When: Last; it is a lot of work for smaller gains.
  - Recommendation: Test in the order design, personalization, price packaging, then device-based pricing, because what moves the needle gets smaller at each step.

### Process

1. Launch a simple paywall: Start with a headline describing the product, a couple of bullet points on what it does and a continue button; focus on getting users into the app.
2. Set up a control: Use the existing paywall as the control and design four to five paywalls to test against it.
3. Test radically different designs: Try video, bullet list, trial timeline and long-form paywalls until one clearly beats the rest.
4. Personalize: Call out the user's name, highlight their goals and show imagery representative of the user's identity.
5. Test price packaging: Test the footer: vertical or horizontal product groups, what goes in each, how the price is presented, a yearly default and only two products.
6. Consider device-based pricing last: Test prices by device, network and battery state only after the bigger levers, because it is a lot of work.
7. Judge results by business type: Check paywall percentage, trial to paid, LTV, D1, D7, D30 and churn, and use ARPU as the key metric only for a quick launch.
8. Be patient: If the first experiment does not produce a statistically significant win, run a second or third.
9. Cover every touch point: Add and test paywalls after onboarding, at feature gates, in settings, on cancellation with a win-back offer and before app deletion.
