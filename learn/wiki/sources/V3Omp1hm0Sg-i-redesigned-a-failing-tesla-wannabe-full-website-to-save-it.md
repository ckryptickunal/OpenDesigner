## Summary

Kole Jain redesigns the website of Fisker, an electric car maker that was going bankrupt, and walks section by section through what was wrong and how he fixed it. He starts by diagnosing the old site: whitespace overused alongside giant text, a sideways label that sticks while scrolling, a split between giant text and small spec text that reads as two different styles, and a page far longer than needed. He then restructures the content (no separate homepage for a one-product company), builds a hero with the headline tucked behind the car, and reworks each section with sliders, a bento layout, hover reveals, a lightbox card and a dealer search bar. For a design system, it gives concrete landing-page rules on hierarchy through proximity, layout variety between sections, image choice and text placement over images, reserving a bright accent for hover, and tucking long but important text behind a 'see more' control.

## Key Ideas

- Diagnose why the existing page fails before redesigning it.
- Too much whitespace combined with giant text makes a page feel disjointed; moving related elements closer together creates hierarchy.
- When giant text above and small spec text below make a page read as two styles, fix the information architecture, not just the type.
- Besides looking good, a website must be structured in a way that makes sense; a page whose only job is to redirect elsewhere is weak structure.
- A company with one product can skip the homepage and lead with that product, keeping other pages in the nav.
- For a full-screen hero, choose a zoomed-out image so the subject does not overwhelm the screen and an info bar does not overlap it.
- Never let text cover an image's focal point; when a small shift no longer clears it, the headline can be placed behind the subject instead.
- Keep images in a set consistent, and move titles off images when visibility over the photo is a worry.
- Introduce a bright accent color only on hover of the call to action so it does not overpower the page.
- Tasteful hover animations, such as an image zooming out while the CTA pops up, add a premium feel.
- Cut sections that repeat images or stats already shown higher on the page.
- Variety in layout between sections is a must; a second features section should not repeat the first one's layout.
- Pick the bento arrangement to fit the images' orientation.
- Replace long accordion text with pictures and paraphrased copy, and tuck lengthy but important text behind a 'see more' control.
- Present an add-on as buttons on the models it applies to, opening a lightbox card, rather than as its own section.
- When stacked sections line up too uniformly, flip one of them to add variety.

### Rules and practices

- **should** (layout, web): Do not overuse whitespace around giant text; push related elements closer together so they form groups. Why: Overused whitespace with giant text makes the page feel disjointed, and grouping creates hierarchy so no element sits alone. [overusing whites space along with the giant text]
- **should** (typography, web): Do not pair giant headline sections with small spec-text sections on the same page; rework the information architecture so the page reads as one style. Why: The size gap almost separates the website into two completely different styles. [separates the website into two completely different styles]
- **should** (patterns, web): Do not make a sideways text label stick to the screen while the user scrolls. Why: Having it stick with you as you scroll is distracting. [having it stick with you as you scroll is kind of distracting]

### Process

1. Diagnose the existing page: Walk through the current site and name why each part is poorly designed: empty homepage, distracting sticky text, overused whitespace with giant text, two clashing text styles, and excess length.
2. Gather inspiration: Review competitors (Tesla, Arrival, Polestar, Lucid, Rivian), pick favorites to borrow from, and also use Dribbble and your own past designs.
3. Restructure the content: Decide what pages are needed; for one product, drop the homepage, lead with the product's perks, keep other pages in the nav, and add mission and blog sections at the bottom.
4. Find the hero image: Search Google Images with Tools set to large images, and pick a zoomed-out shot so the subject does not overwhelm the screen and the info bar will not overlap it.
5. Build text behind the subject: Trace the top edge of the subject, position the headline, then subtract the traced shape so the text appears behind the subject.
6. Fix the header: Remove the side element (caption: 'side rimen'), place the logo at the top and collapse the links into a hamburger; move information from lower on the page up into an info bar in the hero.
7. Rebuild each section in order: For each section, remove repeated content, tighten text sizes, choose a layout that differs from the previous section (slider, bento, columns), and use images and short copy instead of long text.
8. Refine color and imagery: Swap mismatched images for consistent ones, keep the bright accent for CTA hover, sample gradients from nearby images for backgrounds, and extend photos by duplicating, flipping and cropping.
9. Finish with CTA, blog and footer: Use a dealer search bar as the final CTA, vary the blog section's layout and heading font, flip one row when things line up too much, and keep the footer when it must hold many links.
10. Compare with the original: Put the original site next to the redesign to show the difference.
