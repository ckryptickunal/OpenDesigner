# Visual hierarchy

## Overview

Darkness signals importance: the most important buttons and headings are darkest, body and subtext progressively lighter.

## Source Mentions

- [[sources/66oOi9OLMCw-why-the-60-30-10-rule-is-ruining-your-ui-designs|Why the 60-30-10 Rule is RUINING Your UI Designs]]: Darkness signals importance: the most important buttons and headings are darkest, body and subtext progressively lighter.
- [[sources/6CC8lLnqa28-6-things-you-probably-need-to-hear-as-a-web-designer|6 Things You Probably Need to Hear (as a web designer)]]: Give visitors one or maybe two things to look at and click on, like the single call to action in Google's and Apple's headers, and remove everything else.

- [[sources/9WVt1CelBfg-the-stupid-simple-way-to-learn-ui-ux-design-in-exactly-10-minutes|The stupid simple way to learn UI/UX design in exactly 10 minutes]]: Hierarchy comes from spacing-based grouping and from color: two 15 px lines differ in prominence because the second is at 55% opacity.
- [[sources/EOcY3hPMQkk-the-7-color-mistakes-that-ruin-your-ui-designs|The 7 Color Mistakes that RUIN your UI Designs]]: Neutral balance works like white space; secondary information such as file size, type and labels can be dark or light gray instead of black.
- [[sources/EcbgbKtOELY-every-ui-ux-concept-explained-in-under-10-minutes|Every UI/UX Concept Explained in Under 10 Minutes]]: Hierarchy comes from contrast in size, position and color; the most important item goes at the top, large and bold, and grouping related elements is another form of hierarchy. It is not an exact science.
- [[sources/HE4rLEQpiXY-how-to-think-like-a-genius-ui-ux-designer|How to think like a GENIUS UI/UX designer]]: Calls to action should be eye-catching and easy to find; listing cards should lead with scan-critical details like location, rating and price.
- [[sources/Ksx9C2-3yMo-the-3-dashboard-ui-flaws-that-give-away-you-ve-never-built-one|The 3 dashboard UI flaws that give away you've NEVER built one]]: Besides strong versus poor hierarchy in a card, there is hierarchy by what you show and hide (progressive disclosure), placed along a spectrum of explicitness.
- [[sources/Lp6ey4AyDzA-8-web-design-hacks-to-actually-make-your-designs-better|8 Web Design Hacks To ACTUALLY Make Your Designs Better]]: Hierarchy is the most common mistake; use size, weight and color for text, and give a header only one primary call to action.
- [[sources/P2ksReDwWkE-website-layouts-to-make-a-professional-website-design-in-2024|Website Layouts To Make A Professional Website Design in 2024]]: The contrast between massive headline text and very small navbar text, with the navbar placed beneath the headline, makes the headline feel important.
- [[sources/PDcQJOPby1k-5-saas-ui-ux-mistakes-that-scream-you-vibe-code|5 SaaS UI/UX mistakes that SCREAM you Vibe Code]]: Size elements by what users care about: plan name smaller, cost per month larger; drop unnecessary elements.
- [[sources/RCneB_MQ7qs-the-one-thing-vibe-coding-cant-fix-about-your-website|The one thing vibe coding CAN’T fix about your website]]: Darkened edges pull attention to the center of the hero, and motion is used to direct the eye to the focal point.
- [[sources/SfX43uIubj4-4-ui-design-hacks-to-kill-boring-designs|4 UI Design Hacks to KILL boring designs]]: Decorative elements should pull attention toward the centered message; one illustration too many tips the design into clutter.
- [[sources/ToJiXPTNnLY-professional-portfolio-breakdown-why-is-theirs-so-much-better|Professional Portfolio Breakdown — Why Is Theirs So Much Better?]]: Visitors see before they read, so the work's visuals should dominate and text should be secondary.
- [[sources/V3Omp1hm0Sg-i-redesigned-a-failing-tesla-wannabe-full-website-to-save-it|I Redesigned a Failing Tesla WANNABE Full Website To SAVE It]]: Hierarchy comes from pushing elements closer together so none sits by itself; a features section that is too spread out also has much too large a difference in text sizes.

- [[sources/c1TvOcKdBVE-the-8-ui-ux-cheat-codes-for-instantly-better-designs|The 8 UI/UX Cheat Codes for INSTANTLY Better Designs]]: Rank information on a card by importance, group related items, and drop labels the layout already implies.
- [[sources/eMMiLeo_UGI-the-4-levels-of-landing-page-ui-ux-design|The 4 Levels of Landing Page UI/UX Design]]: Level one has flat hierarchy in type and navigation; level three builds clear hierarchy through color and size, slightly overdone so the headline dominates the sub-line.
- [[sources/eks-skills-apple-design-skill-emilkowalski-skills-skills-apple-design-skill-md|emilkowalski/skills: skills/apple-design/SKILL.md]]: Build hierarchy with order, spacing and contrast so the most important thing is the most obvious, and in type with weight, size and leading as a set.
- [[sources/gKM6b2EnW1k-upgrading-9-crucial-ui-elements-free-figma-file-included|Upgrading 9 CRUCIAL UI Elements (free figma file included)]]: Moving a pricing button up throws off hierarchy, which is fixed by making it stroke-only; the price area is separated from features with a tinted background.

- [[sources/lkKGQVHrXzE-designing-with-claude-code|Designing with Claude Code]]: Keep the nav button smaller so it does not compete with the hero button; set supporting text and stat labels in gray 600; drop unnecessary section titles, background tints and background cards.
