Chapter 3

The lighthouse

The keeper’s daughter counted ships by their lamps, not their sails. Sails could lie lied; a lamp told you who was hungry and who was late.

Salt got into everything: hinges, bread, pages.

Her father kept the log in pencil, because ink ran in the damp, and every night he wrote the same three words before the weather: lit, trimmed, watched.

She read it the way other children read the Bible, for the names of ships and the order they came in.

In March the lamp failed twice. The second time she trimmed the wick herself, burned two fingers, and wrote nothing about it in the log.

The inspector came in May with a ledger of his own. He read the pencil entries back to her father one by one, like charges, and stopped at the night in March where the hand was hers.

“Who trimmed this?” he asked, and nobody answered.
