# U5 usability test: student persona (Riya, 14)

Tester: session "U5 student", 2026-09-24. Lane U5 (usability).

## Setup
- **Persona.** Riya, 14, is building a website for her school robotics club with a free AI chatbot. She knows no design or code words, types short messages and loses patience with long text. She has a club logo (a PNG a friend drew) and wants "blue and a bit fun".
- **Host simulated.** The Claude skill with code execution, following `skills/opendesigner/SKILL.md` and its references message by message. Every engine and journey command below was really run, in a scratch project outside the repo. The stand-in logo is a 512 px PNG, measured at 47.7% `#2563EB` blue and 2.5% `#FF9900` orange.
- **Path.** Greeting and log consent, the 5-question sketch (zoom 0), one zoom-in (Color, level 2), then stop and run the Finish steps. Afterwards: `journey.py report`, `engine.py review`, and a read of DESIGN.md.
- **Limits.** Timings in the journey log are compressed, because both roles ran in one sitting. Riya's reactions are the tester's judgment. Claims about hosts and plans are marked [inferred].

## What worked well
- **The greeting and first questions were short.** M3, M5, M6 and M10 were under 35 words, and each asked exactly one thing. She answered each in one to five words.
- **The sketch really built everything from 5 answers.** One command gave tokens, CSS, Tailwind, DESIGN.md, PRODUCT.md and a preview with no errors, and the accessibility floors held (168 color pairs checked in every mode).
- **Answers from her logo worked once recorded.** `keep-hex` pinned her exact blue, and `raw.secondaryColors` turned the logo orange into a proper ramp with contrast-safe text. The orange was the moment she enjoyed most.
- **The "just do it" path was respected.** After one confused question, the model delegated the rest, stayed polite and finished the level in one message. Decisions record `delegated` with a reason, so a teammate can see what she didn't choose.
- **The plain glossary sentences fit a 14-year-old**, for example the accent color and neutral sentences. The trouble was the extra Code line (F17), not the plain sentence.
- **Consent mechanics were correct.** Nothing was logged before her "ok", `share-consent never` deleted the unsent reports, and the sharing question was asked once and never again.
- **The journey report found the right speed-up** despite its attribution bug: "Q-color-09: put the explanation into the question itself", and auto-apply the low-impact color defaults. Both match what she felt.
- **The feedback loop was quiet and safe.** Four `engine.py feedback` notes built pre-filled issue links. Nothing was posted, and no project details were included.
